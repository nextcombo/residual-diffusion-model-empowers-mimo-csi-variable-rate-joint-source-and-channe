Residual Diffusion Model Empowers MIMO CSI Varying Rate Joint Source-Channel Coding, Achieving Substantial Performance Improvement
Abstract: Despite significant progress in channel state information (CSI) compression based on deep learning, several key limitations remain unresolved. Current methods largely treat CSI compression as a pure source coding problem, overlooking the impact of errors during transmission; traditional separate source and channel coding suffer from cliff effects, significantly degrading reconstruction performance under complex channel conditions. While existing autoencoder-based compression schemes can directly extend to joint source and channel coding (JSCC), they struggle to capture complex channel distribution characteristics and exhibit poor scalability with increased model parameters. To overcome the inherent shortcomings of autoencoder methods, this paper proposes a Residual Diffusion Joint Source-Channel Coding framework (RD-JSCC). This framework combines a lightweight autoencoder with a residual diffusion module to achieve iterative optimization of CSI reconstruction. Its flexible decoding strategy dynamically switches between low-complexity autoencoder decoding and high-precision diffusion base reconstruction based on channel conditions, balancing computational efficiency with reconstruction performance. Extensive simulation experiments demonstrate that RD-JSCC significantly outperforms existing autoencoder-based methods in complex wireless environments. Additionally, the framework features several practical capabilities: support for two-step diffusion processes at low latency in the inference phase, single-model compatibility with various compression rates, robustness against fixed bit quantization, and adaptability to non-ideal channel estimation scenarios.
Introduction: With the continuous growth of data transmission volumes, large-scale multi-input multi-output (MIMO) technology has become a core technology for network expansion in next-generation wireless networks. Base stations can achieve high throughput communication under non-ideal channel conditions by deploying large antenna arrays, which requires precise channel state information (CSI) to support effective precoding for downlink transmission. In frequency division duplex (FDD) systems, uplink CSI can be obtained through channel estimation, while downlink CSI needs to be efficiently feedback from user devices to the base station.
Deep learning has significantly advanced the development of various fields in physical layer communication, including nonlinear channel code design, neural channel decoding, and MIMO channel estimation. This paper focuses on the lossy compression of channel state information (CSI) within the physical layer. Traditional compression techniques such as Compressed Sensing have not performed well due to the lack of inherent sparsity in CSI structures, making deep learning a superior choice. Neural lossy compression techniques based on neural networks (i.e., neural lossy compression) have gained significant attention in areas such as image and video compression. In recent years, this method has been used to enhance the efficiency of CSI compression—represented by CSINet, which achieved performance breakthroughs over the mainstream compression-sensing methods through convolutional neural networks. A series of subsequent studies further optimized the neural network-based CSI compression technology, adapting it for joint source-channel coding with CSI.
The introduction of diffusion models has brought about dual breakthroughs in compression efficiency and reconstruction quality for neural image compression. These models, initially used to generate new images based on conditional variables, have rapidly been applied to image compression. Research has demonstrated the superiority of diffusion models in compression tasks and provided new ideas for compression technologies. Although diffusion models have been widely studied in image compression, their potential in CSI compression has not been fully exploited. Existing research has proposed a framework for CSI compression based on generative diffusion, proving its significant superiority over traditional autoencoder methods in modeling complex channels. Building on this research, this paper further optimizes the diffusion-based CSI compression from two key dimensions: firstly, by balancing output diversity to improve reconstruction fidelity, shifting the model objective from generating new samples to maximizing reconstruction accuracy; secondly, using nested representation learning methods to capture multiple compression levels within a unified hidden space. The method in this paper uses scalar quantization to process continuous value latent variables directly mapping to subcarriers subject to power constraints, naturally adapting to noisy transmissions and achieving smooth and adaptive compression rate adjustments according to bandwidth constraints dynamically changing.
This paper proposes a novel variable rate CSI compression framework based on residual diffusion, specifically designed for the efficient compression of CSI matrices in large-scale MIMO systems. The encoder of this framework employs a low-complexity convolutional neural network architecture, while the receiver side adopts a two-stage decoding process, which includes an initial reconstruction phase based on convolutional neural networks and a diffusion optimization module based on U-Net to iteratively improve the quality of CSI reconstruction. The main contributions of this study are as follows: the introduction of the RD-JSCC flexible dual-stage framework, which initializes the back-propagation process with a rough estimate value from the autoencoder and iteratively optimizes the residual error, supporting adaptive switching between lightweight autoencoder decoding and two-step diffusion-based base reconstruction, balancing reconstruction quality with low latency inference requirements; the adoption of a nested representation learning framework based on continuous value hidden space, allowing a single model to support multiple compression rates without the need for retraining or switching models, achieving fine-grained bandwidth adaptation; comparing RD-JSCC with current mainstream deep learning-based JSC schemes using the COST2100 outdoor dataset and 3GPP indoor dataset, demonstrating its performance improvement by orders of magnitude compared to non-diffusion baseline models, with particularly significant effects in complex statistical channel distributions; conducting comprehensive ablation experiments to quantify the contributions of residual diffusion design, encoder complexity, and adaptive SNR to performance, systematically analyzing the trade-off between complexity and performance under different channel scenarios, and clarifying the applicability of diffusion-based reconstruction in various scenarios; addressing practical deployment needs, validating that the improvement in normalized mean squared error (NMSE) can significantly reduce block error rate (BLER), proving the robustness of RD-JSCC against non-ideal channel estimation, and that its performance degradation is negligible even at low bit-quantization levels of 4 bits per dimension.
Method Introduction: The proposed RD-JSCC framework is designed for CSI compression in large-scale MIMO systems. Its core is a two-stage architecture consisting of a lightweight autoencoder combined with residual diffusion modules, while also integrating nested representation learning to achieve variable rate compression. The overall method revolves around four core modules: system modeling, basic autoencoder design, residual diffusion optimization, and variable rate compression. Key designs and implementations are as follows:
1  
System Modeling: For large-scale MIMO systems in Frequency Division Duplex (FDD) mode, the system is simplified to a Single Input Multiple Output (SIMO) configuration (user-end single antenna), where downlink CSI is represented as a complex value matrix. The encoder compresses it into a fixed-length complex value latent vector, with the compression rate determined by the ratio of the latent vector dimension to the CSI matrix dimension; the receiver processes in two stages. First, the autoencoder decoder obtains an approximate estimate of CSI, then through residual diffusion model iteration and noise reduction optimization, the model parameters are optimized using normalized mean squared error (NMSE) as the distorted metric.
2  
Low-Complexity Self-Encoder Design: The encoder employs a lightweight convolutional neural network with a fully connected layer architecture, incorporating an adaptive module for signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) dynamically scaling feature activation values to enhance robustness under varying SNR conditions. The decoder utilizes a deeper architecture with residual blocks, and mirror encoders incorporate an SNR adaptive module. End-to-end training is performed using the mean squared error (MSE) loss function, employing continuous value hidden representations instead of vector quantization to avoid cliff effects, achieving performance smooth decay and adapting to joint source and channel coding.
3  
Residual Diffusion CSI Optimization: First, convert CSI from the spatial domain to the angle-delay domain and truncate it, utilizing its sparsity to reduce computational complexity. In the first stage of the receiver, an autoencoder obtains a rough estimate of CSI, and in the second stage, initializes the back-propagation process with this estimate (different from traditional initialization with Gaussian noise), employing U-Net as the backbone network for diffusion denoising. The reconstruction value of CSI is iteratively optimized through residual diffusion formulas. The model adopts an x-predicted diffusion loss function, supporting few-step diffusion inference; training is divided into two stages: first, train the autoencoder, then freeze its weights to train the diffusion denoising network, and adaptive selection between single-stage (only autoencoder) or dual-stage (autoencoder + diffusion) decoding can be made based on uplink channel quality.
4  
Variable Rate Compression Implementation: Based on the MRL framework, learning hierarchical nested latent representations within a single model, achieving various compression rates by truncation of hidden vectors to different lengths without training multiple models. Extend the loss function to be the weighted sum of reconstruction error at each compression level, achieving joint optimization for multiple compression rates. The single model can support discrete compression levels and all intermediate levels, and adaptively selects the dimension of hidden vectors during inference based on available bandwidth to eliminate the overhead of model switching.
5  
Practical Design: Introduces μ-law compression dispersion combined with uniform scalar quantization to achieve fixed bit representation of hidden vectors, where performance degradation under 4-bit/dimension quantization is negligible; incorporates channel estimation error into the training process to enhance the model's robustness against non-ideal CSI; adds an SNR adaptive module, allowing the model to adjust reconstruction strategies based on channel quality, further optimizing performance.
The conclusion of this paper proposes a hybrid joint source-channel coding scheme for MIMO channel state information (CSI) compression, which combines the initial estimation by an autoencoder with reconstruction optimization based on diffusion. The residual diffusion method in this paper initializes the back-diffusion process with the rough estimation values of the autoencoder, specifically designed for the CSI reconstruction task. This two-stage framework can terminate decoding at any stage according to channel conditions and performance requirements, achieving a balance between computational complexity and reconstruction fidelity. Additionally, leveraging x-predictive technology, this paper realizes low-complexity two-step diffusion inference with minimal performance loss, significantly reducing inference latency.
Experimental results across varying channel complexity have shown that diffusion-based reconstruction optimization performs optimally in channels with complex statistical characteristics, while the autoencoder approach can efficiently adapt to simple channel distributions. These findings demonstrate that under complex uplink channel conditions, diffusion models are promising for enhancing base station-side CSI reconstruction performance.
Future research directions include: further optimizing multirate compression techniques within a single model; delving into the impact of quantization effects on model performance; exploring weight quantization techniques to enhance model lightweightness; and optimizing the U-Net backbone network to improve computational efficiency while maintaining performance. Moreover, introducing additional loss functions (such as binary cross-entropy losses between transmitted and received data, and effective MIMO channel capacity losses) directly targeting downstream tasks for optimization is also highly researchable.
